# Document Type Classification Reference

This reference helps identify the correct document type so the right summary template is applied.

---

## Academic / Research

**Identifiers:**
- Has an "Abstract" section
- Lists authors with affiliations
- Contains "Introduction", "Methodology", "Results", "Discussion", "Conclusion", "References"
- Published in or submitted to a journal, conference, or institution
- Heavy use of citations (e.g., [1], (Smith, 2020))
- Statistical language: p-value, confidence interval, sample size, correlation

**Examples:** Journal articles, preprints (arXiv), theses, dissertations, clinical study reports

---

## Business / Report

**Identifiers:**
- Has an "Executive Summary"
- Produced by a company, consultancy, NGO, or government agency
- Contains sections like "Market Overview", "Findings", "Recommendations", "Appendix"
- May include charts, graphs, tables of financial/operational data
- Aimed at decision-makers

**Examples:** McKinsey reports, annual reports, earnings releases, investment memos, policy briefs, sustainability reports, RFPs

---

## Legal / Contract

**Identifiers:**
- Uses defined terms in ALL CAPS or "defined terms" (e.g., "Licensor", "Agreement")
- Has numbered/lettered clauses and sub-clauses
- Language like "hereby", "whereas", "notwithstanding", "indemnify", "warranty"
- Lists obligations of parties
- Has signature blocks

**Examples:** NDAs, service agreements, terms of service, lease agreements, employment contracts, settlement agreements, privacy policies

---

## Technical / Manual

**Identifiers:**
- Instructions written in imperative form ("Click X", "Run Y", "Configure Z")
- Contains code snippets, command-line examples, or configuration syntax
- Has version numbers, system requirements, compatibility tables
- Organized around features, APIs, or procedures
- Numbered steps

**Examples:** User guides, API documentation, developer docs, installation guides, spec sheets, system requirements documents, release notes

---

## Narrative / Book

**Identifiers:**
- Long-form prose with chapters or parts
- Has a table of contents with descriptive chapter names
- Authored by one or a few individuals
- May be argumentative, journalistic, memoir-style, or story-driven

**Examples:** Business books, self-help books, biographies, essay collections, long-form investigative journalism

---

## General / Other

Use this when the document doesn't clearly fit another category. Examples:
- Meeting minutes
- Course syllabi
- Presentations exported to PDF
- Newsletters
- Forms

---

## Mixed Documents

Some documents span multiple types. Use your judgment:
- A **corporate research report** that reads like an academic paper → Business/Report
- A **government regulation** that reads like a contract → Legal
- A **technical whitepaper** from a vendor → Business (with Technical sections noted)

When in doubt, pick the type that best matches the *primary audience* and *primary purpose*.
